2. Speaking Section

These questions present a scenario or a question, and you must choose the most appropriate and professional response, often using Standard Marine Communication Phrases (SMCP).

Example Question: Will the tug boat be ready for our arrival at 1200?





3. Reading Section

You will read a passage (e.g., an email, a technical report, or a regulatory excerpt) and then answer questions based on its content.

Example Reading Passage:

The International Maritime Organization (IMO) serves as the specialized agency of the United Nations responsible for the safety and security of shipping and the prevention of marine pollution by ships. To maintain these standards, several key international conventions have been established. SOLAS (Safety of Life at Sea) remains the most important treaty concerning the safety of merchant ships. To protect the environment, MARPOL regulates various forms of marine pollution, including oil, sewage, and garbage. For the crew, the STCW convention (Standards of Training, Certification, and Watchkeeping) ensures that all seafarers meet global requirements for competence and safety. Additionally, the MLC 2006 (Maritime Labour Convention) sets out seafarers' rights to decent conditions of work, including health, safety, and living standards. Ships are also required to implement a Safety Management System (SMS) under the ISM Code to ensure safe operations at every level.

Example Question: According to the passage and maritime regulations, which convention is specifically designed to establish global standards for the training and certification of seafarers?





4. Writing Section

These questions test your grammatical precision and ability to complete maritime documents or correspondence. You might need to choose the correct word or phrase to fill a blank.

Example Question: The crew members are responsible for the regular ______ of all life-saving appliances on board.





ATMEP - AMCOL Test of Maritime English Proficiency Structure

The examination consists of four core skills, totaling 120 multiple-choice and performance-based items.

1. Listening Skill (30 Items)

  • VHF Communication: Understanding ship-to-shore and ship-to-ship radio exchanges.
  • Operational Talks: Comprehending safety briefings, weather reports, and internal engine room/bridge commands.

2. Speaking Skill (30 Items)

  • Question-Response: Providing immediate, clear answers to technical and safety-related inquiries.
  • SMCP Simulation: Completing professional dialogues using Standard Marine Communication Phrases in simulated environments.

3. Reading Skill (30 Items)

  • Technical Information: Scanning manuals, diagrams, and machinery specifications for specific data.
  • Regulatory Literacy: Interpreting main ideas from international codes such as SOLAS, MARPOL, and IMDG.

4. Writing Skill (30 Items)

  • Grammatical Precision: Completing sentences with correct maritime terminology and technical parts of speech.
  • Professional Documentation: Ability to identify and restore missing critical data in pre-formatted maritime documents, accurate logbook entries, incident reports, and formal maritime correspondence.
